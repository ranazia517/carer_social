## 3. Product / How it works — the fuller story

*Intent: the considered read for someone who wants to understand what CARER really is before trusting it. Six honest movements.*

### A companion, not a service desk

> Most things aimed at carers are about *tasks* — rotas, medications, appointments, forms. CARER is deliberately not that. It's for the part of caring no one asks about: the inner life of the person doing it. When you open CARER, it doesn't greet you with a form or a goal to set. It opens mid-relationship, ready to listen. It reflects back what you're feeling, holds it, and — only if you want — gently widens the view. It meets the hard feeling first, every time.

### It remembers your own words — and never a verdict

> The thing carers tell us they'd value most is not having to explain from the beginning every time. So CARER remembers — but only ever *your* words, exactly as you said them, with when you said them. It might say, *"A while ago you mentioned your mum's fall — how are things now?"* That's something you once told it, offered back as a gentle prompt. It is never a conclusion CARER has drawn about you. There is no hidden profile, no "pattern" it has spotted, no assessment of how you're doing. Your memory is yours: you can see all of it, edit it, and take it with you.

### Grief that has no ending — held, not fixed

> Caring often means grieving someone who is still alive — the person they used to be, the future you'd imagined, the version of yourself you had to set aside. CARER treats that grief as real and names it plainly. It holds the "both/and" — that you can love someone deeply *and* grieve them at the same time; feel relief *and* devastation in the same breath. It won't push you toward "acceptance," "closure," or "moving on," because some griefs don't work that way, and being told they should only wounds twice. And it doesn't disappear when the caring ends. If you're in the after, CARER is still here — holding the whole story as something that mattered.

### Four-directional crisis and safeguarding — outside the model, always on

> Some moments need more than a companion. CARER's safety layer is built to catch four kinds of hard moment: when *you* feel unsafe or don't want to go on; when you're at breaking point; when you're frightened of what the strain might lead you to do to the person you care for; and when the person you care for is hurting *you*. In every case, CARER responds without shock and without judgement — it validates the *feeling*, never an *act* — and puts the right route one tap away. It won't tell you "this is abuse" or "this is neglect"; it isn't an assessor, and it will be honest that it can't report anything on your behalf. Crucially, this layer sits **outside** the conversation — it works even if the companion is offline, unavailable, or you've never signed in. Help is never paywalled and never more than a tap away.

### Signposting to real, human help

> CARER is clear about what it is and isn't. When something needs a professional — a clinician, a helpline, a specialist charity — it says so, and points you there. It will never pretend to be your only support. Feeling less alone should lead you *toward* people, not away from them, so CARER points back to human connection: Carers UK, condition-specific charities, peer communities, and the people in your life.

### On your device, and private by design

> The first time you use CARER, it downloads its companion to your phone over Wi-Fi — once. After that, your conversations run **on your own device**, offline, free. What you say doesn't need to leave your phone at all. If you choose to back things up, that backup is end-to-end encrypted, so even we can't read it. Cloud assistance is switched **off** unless you turn it on. The person you care for is never a subject in CARER — never profiled, tracked, or monitored. This isn't a promise we ask you to take on trust; it's how the product is built.

> There is no single kind of carer. CARER was designed, from the start, against six real carer situations — so that whoever you are, it meets you where you actually are.

**The sandwich carer**
> Caring for an ageing parent while raising children and holding down a job — pulled apart, time-poor, and only able to feel any of it once everyone else is finally asleep. CARER is built for minutes, not sessions, and picks up exactly where you left off.

**The spousal carer**
> Walking the treatment rollercoaster beside a partner — being "the strong one," with your own fear tucked away where they can't see it. CARER is somewhere to put that fear down without being told it'll all be fine.

**The parent of a disabled or autistic child**
> Loving your child completely, fighting the systems that are meant to help, and quietly carrying a grief that has no finish line. CARER holds that "both/and" — love and sorrow together — and never pushes you toward "closure."

**The carer of an adult child with a serious mental illness**
> Living with vigilance, stigma, and the fear of the next phone call — carrying love, fear, and anger all braided together. CARER gives that churn a safe, non-judging outlet, and never turns your feelings into something to manage for someone else's sake.

**The young adult carer**
> Helping look after someone at home while trying to be a young person too — often unseen, often without even the word for what you are. CARER offers recognition, a place for the resentment-and-love, and support that holds both parts of who you are. *(CARER's core companion is for ages 18 and over; younger carers are signposted to dedicated young-carer services.)*

**The former carer, in the after**
> When the caring ends, most support falls away — but you don't stop needing to be held. CARER doesn't disappear at that moment. It stays, holds the guilt of relief and the loss of the role, and remembers the whole story as something that mattered.

**Closing line:** *You don't have to fit one of these exactly. If you're looking after someone, CARER is for you.*

## 6. Our approach / principles

*Intent: the constitution as public-facing promises — the things CARER will never do, stated plainly and proudly.*

**Heading:** **The promises we build in, not just intend.**

> These aren't good intentions — they're built into how CARER works, so that even we couldn't break them.

**We never score you.**
> There is no burnout meter, no stress level, no mood number, no "how you're doing" gauge — anywhere. You're a person under strain, not a patient to be measured. CARER holds the weight; it never weighs it.

**Your words stay yours.**
> What you say lives on your device, in your own words. We never sell it, never train AI on it, and never turn it into a verdict about you. Your memory is something *you* said — never something we decided.

**We never watch the person you care for.**
> The person you care for is never profiled, tracked, or monitored in CARER. We don't advise on their condition, medications, or care — we'll always point you to the people who can. Their health has no place in our system at all.

**We won't add to your to-do list.**
> No rotas, no task lists, no medication trackers, no chores to log. You're already drowning in coordination. CARER's job is to *subtract* load, never add it.

**No streaks, no guilt, no 3am pull.**
> There's nothing to keep up, nothing to break, and nothing to miss. Success isn't how long you stay — it's whether you felt held enough to put the phone down. Late at night, CARER helps you rest, never keeps you scrolling.

**Help is one tap away — even offline.**
> The crisis and safeguarding route is free forever, works with no signal, and is reachable before you've even signed in. Safety is never behind a paywall or a login.

**Non-clinical by design.**
> CARER does not diagnose, treat, assess, or monitor anyone. It's a wellbeing companion — it supports, reflects, remembers, holds, and signposts. When something is clinical, it tells you honestly and points you to the right people.

## 8. Safety / getting urgent help

**Banner (calm, not alarming):**
> **CARER is not an emergency or medical service.** It's a companion. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, please use the services below — they're staffed by people trained to help, and they're free.

### How CARER helps in a hard moment

> CARER's crisis and safeguarding support is designed to be there when things feel frightening — for you, or for the person you care for. It's built to be **free, one tap away on every screen, and available even offline and before you've signed in.** It works whether or not the companion is running.
>
> It supports four kinds of hard moment:
> 1. When you feel unsafe, or don't want to go on.
> 2. When you're at breaking point and can't do this anymore.
> 3. When the strain has you frightened of what you might do to the person you care for.
> 4. When the person you care for is hurting you.
>
> In every case, CARER responds without shock or judgement. It holds the *feeling*, never validates a harmful *act*, and puts the right route one tap away. It won't label anything "abuse" or "neglect," it isn't an assessor, and it will be honest that it can't contact anyone on your behalf. If you can't speak aloud because someone's nearby, it offers a **text route first**.

### Urgent UK resources

> **In an emergency — someone is in immediate danger:**
> - **999** (if you can't speak, call 999 then press **55** on a mobile to use the Silent Solution)
>
> **If you're struggling to cope or don't feel safe:**
> - **Samaritans** — **116 123** (free, 24/7, call)
> - **Shout** — text **SHOUT** to **85258** (free, 24/7, text)
> - **NHS 111**, mental-health option (option **2**) — 24/7; or text **07860 009642**
>
> **Carer-specific support:**
> - **Carers UK helpline** — **0808 808 7777** (Mon–Fri, 9am–6pm) *(please pair with a 24/7 line out of hours)*
>
> **If you're worried about the safety of the person you care for, or your own:**
> - **Adult safeguarding** — contact your **local council** (under the Care Act 2014, anyone, including you, can raise a concern)
> - **National Domestic Abuse Helpline** — **0808 2000 247** (free, 24/7)
>
> **If you're a young carer (under 18):**
> - **Childline** — **0800 1111** (free, 24/7, and free from any phone)

## 11. FAQ

*Intent: 10–15 real, plain answers to the questions carers actually ask.*

**Is CARER therapy or counselling?**
> No. CARER is a warm, non-clinical companion — somewhere to be heard and to hold difficult feelings. It doesn't diagnose, treat, or assess anyone. When you need a therapist, a clinician, or a crisis service, CARER will point you to the right people.

**Is my data really private?**
> Yes — it's the whole point. By default CARER runs on your own device, so your words can stay on your phone. Any backup is end-to-end encrypted so even we can't read it. We never sell your data or train AI on it, and it never reaches your family or the person you care for.

**Does it work offline?**
> Yes. After a one-time download over Wi-Fi, the companion runs on your device — offline. And the urgent-help route works with no signal at all, even before you've signed in.

**Is it free?**
> The crisis and safeguarding support is **free, always** — that will never be behind a paywall. `[PLACEHOLDER: confirm the pricing model for the rest of the app — free, freemium, subscription, or B2B2C via employers/charities.]`

**Who is it for?**
> Anyone looking after a family member or friend — a partner, parent, child, or someone close. Whether you're a sandwich carer, a spouse, a parent of a disabled child, caring for someone with a mental illness, a young adult carer, or someone whose caring has ended. You don't have to call yourself a "carer" to belong here.

**Does it track or monitor the person I care for?**
> No — never. The person you care for is never profiled, tracked, or monitored in CARER. We don't hold their condition, medications, or care as data, and we don't advise on any of it. We'll always point you to the people who can.

**Do I need a wearable or any special device?**
> No. CARER is just an app on your phone. No wearables, no sensors, no set-up kit.

**Are there streaks, reminders, or pressure to keep coming back?**
> No. There are no streaks, no badges, no day-counters, and no guilt-trip notifications. Success isn't how long you stay — it's whether you felt held enough to put the phone down. Late at night, CARER helps you rest rather than keeping you scrolling.

**Does CARER score or rate how I'm doing?**
> No. There's no burnout meter, stress level, mood number, or "how you're doing" gauge anywhere. You're a person under strain, not a patient to be measured.

**What happens in a crisis?**
> A calm, one-tap crisis and safeguarding route is on every screen — free, offline, and available before sign-in. It supports four kinds of hard moment (your own safety, breaking point, fear of harm to the person you care for, and harm being done to you), always without judgement, and always pointing you to trained human help. CARER is honest that it isn't a crisis service and can't contact anyone on your behalf.

**Can I delete everything?**
> Yes. You can view, edit, export, or permanently delete everything CARER remembers, whenever you like — without losing access to the companion.

**Will it remember what I told it last time?**
> Yes — that's one of the best things about it. CARER holds on to what *you* said, in your own words, so you never have to start over. It offers your words back gently, and it's always something you told it — never a conclusion it drew about you.

**Can it give me advice about my relative's illness or medication?**
> No — that's a firm line. CARER doesn't advise on anyone's condition, medication, symptoms, or care. It'll hold how you *feel* about it, and point you to the clinicians and charities who can help with the rest.

**I'm not sure I even count as a carer. Is it still for me?**
> Yes. Many people caring for someone don't think of themselves as "a carer" at all. If you're helping look after someone, CARER is for you.

**Does it stay with me if the caring ends?**
> Yes. CARER doesn't disappear when the caring does. It holds the after — the grief, the relief and its guilt, the question of who you are now — and remembers the whole story as something that mattered.

**Is there an age limit?**
> The core companion is for ages 18 and over. If you're a young carer under 18, CARER will point you to dedicated young-carer services built for you.
